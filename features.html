Features

Everything the app does, and exactly which TikTok API each part uses.

Account connection

You connect your TikTok account through Login Kit. The app opens TikTok's own authorization page in your browser and receives an access token back through a local callback address. Authorization uses OAuth 2.0 with PKCE.

The app requests four scopes:

user.info.basicYour open ID and display name, so the dashboard can show which account is connected and attach each upload to the right account.
user.info.statsFollower count, total likes and video count, for your growth chart.
video.listYour own videos and their view, like and comment counts, for the per-video performance table.
video.publishPublishing a video to your own profile through Direct Post.

Your TikTok password is never entered into the app and is never stored. Only the access token is kept, in a file on your own computer.

Title and hashtag drafting

When a new video file appears in your folder, the app generates a draft title and a set of hashtags from the file name and rotates between several opening hooks so your posts do not all read the same way. The draft is a starting point — you can edit both before publishing.

Privacy level, taken from your account

Before showing you the publish form, the app calls TikTok's Creator Info endpoint and offers only the privacy options that TikTok reports for your account. You choose the level for every post. There is no default that publishes without you selecting one.

Interaction and commercial content settings

Comments, Duet and Stitch can each be enabled or disabled per post. If a video is branded content or a paid partnership, you mark it, and the app passes the disclosure flags to TikTok exactly as you set them. TikTok's own rule — that paid-partnership content must be public — is shown next to the toggles.

Scheduled publishing

You set the times of day you want to post, for example 19:00 and 22:00. New videos are queued into the next free slot. A background worker checks the queue, publishes what is due through the Content Posting API, then polls the status endpoint until TikTok confirms the post is complete. Both success and failure are written to a log you can read.

Publishing is rate-limited by the app itself so it stays inside TikTok's request limits.

Performance dashboard

Twice a day the app reads your own account statistics and your own video list through the Display API and stores them locally. The dashboard charts followers, views, likes and comments over time so you can see which posting times work.

Only your own account is ever read. The app has no way to look at anyone else's data.

Notifications

Optionally, the app can send you a message when a video is queued, published, or fails. This is off unless you turn it on and supply your own notification token.
